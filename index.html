More about this bond
  • Toronto-Dominion Bank. Pays 5.70% a year on face value, in Singapore dollars, every six months.
  • The bank can choose to repay you on 31 July 2029. If it does not, the rate resets to a Singapore interest rate plus 2.65%, and it can repay you at later dates.
  • TD can skip a payment, but if it does, it cannot pay its own shareholders until it pays you again.
  • If TD were ever failing, this bond turns into TD shares rather than disappearing.
  • TD's capital cushion was 14.3% at April 2026, well above what regulators require.

Source: TD pricing supplement dated 8 July 2024 (Series 2023-9) and TD's programme prospectus, July 2026.

One fifth, no more

Five rules

  1. Never sell a bond because its price fell. You still get every coupon.
  2. Before buying any bond, ask a second bank for a price on the same bond. Only deal if the prices are close. Why: banks add a hidden margin to bond prices; a second quote shows you how much.
  3. Ask for the yield to call (or yield to maturity), not the coupon rate. The coupon is what the bond pays on 100; the yield is what you actually earn on the price you pay. Buy on the yield. Why: a 5.7% coupon bought at 104 earns about 4.5% a year to the call date.
  4. Keep the bottom tier at one fifth. Only buy more perpetuals when a local bank issues a new one, never at a price above 100.
  5. Each year, check your spending against the cost-of-living line above.

The ten-year projection is simplified:

  • Yields stay at today's levels for all ten years.
  • Coupons you do not spend are reinvested at the same yield.
  • CPF is treated as part of the pool earning its 4%.
  • No tax: Singapore does not tax individuals on bond interest or CPF.
  • No other income (rent, dividends, pensions) is counted.
